# Building your call for speakers

This is the form the public fills in to propose a talk, workshop, or panel. Find it under
**Forms** in the sidebar.

## Creating a form

Click **Add → Create Form** (or **Copy from…** to clone an existing form, questions and all —
handy if you're running a second event and want the same setup). The form builder opens as a
short wizard with a step list down the left and a tick against each step once it's filled in.

### Step 1 — What are you collecting?

Choose **Abstracts** (short proposals reviewed before sessions are finalised) or **Sessions**
(full proposals with all the details up front). You can also toggle on a step that collects
speaker/participant contact details. You can change these choices later.

### Step 2 — Welcome screen

The page a visitor sees before they start filling anything in:

- **Internal Form Name** — for your own reference, not shown publicly.
- **External Form Title** and **Page Heading** — what visitors actually see (the heading is
  short — 15 characters).
- **Welcome Message** — a rich-text field for your intro copy: what you're looking for, tracks,
  links to your speaker agreement or FAQ, key dates. Use the toolbar for headings, bold, links,
  lists and images.

### Step 3 — Abstract questions

The questions about the talk itself. A sensible starting set is already there — Title,
Description, Format, Tags, Track, Level, Language — and you can add more with **+ Add Field**,
either picking from your existing question library or creating a brand-new one.

Each question can be marked **Required** or left optional, and a few "locked" fields (like Title)
can't be removed because the rest of the app depends on them.

**Conditional questions.** Open a question's **⋯** menu → **Conditional logic** to make it appear
only when an earlier answer matches — for example, show "Room setup requirements" only when
**Format** is "Workshop". This keeps the form short for everyone else. You can only refer to
questions that come earlier in the form, which avoids the form asking about its own answer.

**Routing.** Also configurable here: rules that look at an answer (typically Track or Format) and
automatically tag the submission, assign it to the right review round, or notify a specific
teammate. For example, everything tagged "Track: Agents" can auto-assign to your Agents track
lead's review queue. Every rule that fires is recorded, so you can always see why a submission
landed where it did.

### Step 4 — Participant questions

What you collect about the people involved — name, email, phone, bio, headshot. You choose which
roles submitters can add (Speaker, Co-speaker, Moderator, Panelist) and can cap how many of each,
or overall.

### Step 5 — Form settings

- **Close date** — when the form stops accepting submissions. Set one to also switch on automatic
  reminder emails to anyone who's left a submission in draft.
- **Submission limits** — cap how many proposals one person can submit to this form (drafts
  count).
- **After submission** — whether visitors are automatically carried through to their speaker
  portal 10 seconds after submitting (with a manual "Continue" button too), and the confirmation
  message they see.

  > **Note:** the wording here matters — this is the moment a first-time submitter finds out what
  > happens next, so make it concrete: what you'll do, and roughly when they'll hear back.

### Step 6 — Notifications

Turn on the **Submission Confirmation** email sent to every submitter (turned on by default —
this one matters, don't disable it), and optionally set which admins get notified when a new
submission arrives or an existing one is edited.

## Publishing and sharing

Use **View Form** to preview it exactly as the public will see it, then **Copy Link** to grab the
public URL to share. The form's row in the **Forms** list shows live counts (submissions, drafts)
and a status chip — **Open** or **Closed**. You can close a form early, or reopen it, from the
row's **⋯** menu.

## What a submitter experiences

Visitors go through a short numbered flow: **Welcome → sign in by email link → fill in the
submission → add participants → review and submit**. There's no password — they enter their
email and get a magic link, the same sign-in mechanism you use as an organiser. If it's their
first time, an account (and speaker-portal access) is created automatically; if they've submitted
before, it picks up where they left off. Answers autosave as they go, so a dropped connection
doesn't lose their work.

After submitting, they land on your confirmation page and receive the confirmation email with a
link straight into their speaker portal.
